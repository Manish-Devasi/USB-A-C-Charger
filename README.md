## Automotive USB A+C Charger

An Electronics and Computer Science Engineering Major Project at St. John College of Engineering & Management, University of Mumbai.

 📌 Project Overview

The **Automotive USB A+C Charger** aims to provide a safe, reliable, and convenient charging solution for electronic devices inside vehicles. The proposed system integrates USB Type-A and USB Type-C charging interfaces and uses a DC–DC power conversion stage to provide regulated output power from the vehicle's electrical supply.

The project focuses on power conversion, voltage regulation, electrical protection, thermal management, and compact automotive design.

## 🎯 Objectives

- Design an automotive charging circuit that converts vehicle input power into regulated USB output power.
- Integrate USB Type-A and USB Type-C charging interfaces.
- Incorporate protection against over-voltage, over-current, short circuits, and excessive temperature.
- Improve power-conversion efficiency and thermal performance.
- Develop a compact and reliable charger prototype.
- Evaluate the system's output voltage, current capability, efficiency, and protection functions.

## ✨ Key Features

- **Dual USB interfaces:** USB Type-A and USB Type-C.
- **Voltage regulation:** Designed to provide stable charging output.
- **Electrical protection:** Planned protection against common electrical faults.
- **Compact design:** Intended for automotive applications.
- **Multiple-device compatibility:** Designed to support compatible devices through both ports, within the system's power limits.
- **Future expandability:** Scope for USB-C Power Delivery (PD), intelligent power sharing, and improved monitoring.

## ⚙️ Working Principle

The proposed system operates through the following stages:

1. **DC Input:** Receives power from the vehicle's electrical supply.
2. **Input Protection:** Helps protect the circuit against electrical disturbances.
3. **DC–DC Conversion:** Converts the input voltage into the required regulated output.
4. **Voltage Regulation and Control:** Maintains the required output conditions.
5. **USB Output:** Supplies charging power through the Type-A and Type-C ports.
6. **Protection:** Protection circuits are intended to respond to over-current, short circuits, over-voltage, and excessive temperature.

## 🔬 Project Methodology

1. Identify automotive input and USB output requirements.
2. Select a suitable DC–DC converter topology and protection components.
3. Design the circuit schematic.
4. Calculate component ratings and prepare the PCB layout.
5. Assemble the prototype PCB and hardware.
6. Test output voltage, current, and charging performance.
7. Evaluate protection functions, temperature, and efficiency.
8. Document the results and validate the design.
